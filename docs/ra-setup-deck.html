    What this guide covers

      First, the science. You will be asked what the study is about, so it helps to have an answer.
      The study

      The question·Where does a price come from?

      People do not read a value off an internal price tag. They build one, while they look, out of what is in front of them and what they brought with them. We are testing three additions to the Price Accumulation model of how that happens.

      • Attribute weights. Not every feature counts equally, and which ones count is personal.
      • Multiple anchors. Your own budget competes with the prices the market is showing you.
      • Rejection thresholds. Saying no is a decision with its own criterion, not just the absence of a yes.
      Eye tracking is how we see the accumulation happening, rather than inferring it from the final number alone.
      The study

      Design·Two tasks, two domains

      Auction task

      12 search episodes| competition: low · high| 6 attributes, fixed| 12 options on market| 90 s timeout

      Continuous / DC task

      attributes: 2 · 4 · 6| choice block + pricing block| 10 pairs (jobs) · 6 (houses)| 15 s / 25 s timeouts
      Session 1 → jobs| Session 2 → houses| odd participant IDs run the two tasks in reverse order
      The attribute-load manipulation lives in the second task only. The auction's trials are too long to afford three levels of it.
      The study

      The two tasks·What the participant actually does

      Auction

      Houses or jobs arrive on a market and expire. The participant inspects them, rejects the ones they do not want with a right-click, and bids on one.

      A cleared bid transacts at the market threshold, not at the bid. That is what makes honest bidding the best strategy.

      Continuous / DC

      The same pair of options is shown twice. Once they choose between them. Later they put a price on each one separately.

      When someone chooses one option but prices the other higher, that is a preference reversal, and it is our headline measure.

      Both tasks run through one participant-management layer, so identity, counterbalancing, crash recovery, and eye tracking work the same way in each.
      The study

      Personalisation·Why two participants see different houses

      Before either task starts, we ask for the participant's own budget (houses) or lowest acceptable wage (jobs), and for how much each attribute matters to them. Both answers feed straight back into what they are shown.

      • The price range of the stimuli is centred on their anchor, so the options are relevant to them rather than to an average person.
      • Their attribute ratings choose which attributes appear at each load level.
      This is by design What has to be constant across participants is the trial counts and the design structure, not the literal items. Compare a memory experiment giving each participant a different word list from the same generator.
      If a participant asks why their houses look cheap or expensive, the honest answer is that the range was built around the budget they gave us.

        Before they arrive

        Rig check·Ten minutes, once per day

        • Open MATLAB and cd to the experiment folder.
        • Run the preflight for today's participant and session number. It prints the resolved plan, the estimated duration, and the path, image, and AOI checks in one pass.
        • Confirm every path comes back present. A missing path is a crash twenty minutes into a session otherwise.
        • Check the eye tracker is connected and the desktop calibration utility sees it.
        • Close everything else. Browsers and Teams notifications land on top of a full-screen Psychtoolbox window.
        %% in MATLAB, from the experiment folder
preflight(12, 1, 'rig', 'lab')   % participant 12, session 1
utils.verifyPaths(utils.config('rig', 'lab'))
utils.checkImages(utils.config('rig', 'lab'), 'houses')
        None of these open a Psychtoolbox window, so you can run them with the participant already in the room if you have to.

          Intake

          Greeting and consent·Before anything is switched on

          1. 01Confirm the booking. Check their name against the REP sign-up and note the participant ID you will use. Never improvise an ID.
          2. 02Walk through consent. Say out loud that we record eye movements, that nothing is video-recorded of their face, and that they can stop at any point without penalty.
          3. 03Ask the screening questions. Glasses, hard contact lenses, and heavy eye make-up all affect tracking. None of them are exclusions on their own. Note them.
          4. 04Phone away, bathroom now. A session runs about 45 minutes and the market clock does not pause.
          5. 05Seat them and set the chair height. Eyes roughly level with the top third of the screen, at the viewing distance recorded in the rig profile.
          The viewing distance is not cosmetic. Every eye-tracking region of interest in the dataset is defined in degrees of visual angle and converted using that number.
          Intake

          Calibration·Two different things with the same name

          Desktop utility · hardware

          Lens angle, lighting, display profile. Done once when the rig is set up. Nothing to do with any particular participant.

          In-task · per participant

          A five-point calibration that runs automatically at the start of each task. A dot shrinks at five screen positions and they follow it with their eyes.

          Both are required The desktop tool does not replace per-participant calibration and vice versa. If calibration does not converge the code warns rather than stopping, so watch the console.

          Tell them: follow the dot, keep your head still, it takes about twenty seconds. Drift checks repeat during the session and need the same thing.

          A participant who nods along to instructions and then moves their head throughout is the single most common cause of unusable gaze data.

            Running it

            The five switches·Top of run_battery.m

            SwitchReal sessionWhat it does
            RIG'lab'Screen geometry, viewing distance, and eye tracking on. 'dev' is a laptop profile and must never produce dataset data.
            TESTINGfalsetrue shortens every block and skips instructions. Useful for a smoke test, never for a participant.
            JOBS_ARM'synthetic'Which prepared job-stimulus set to load. Leave it unless told otherwise.
            TRACEfalsetrue prints a console line at every checkpoint. Turn it on when chasing a crash.
            FORCE_DOMAIN''Overrides the schedule and forces every run onto one domain. Quick testing only.
            The two that silently ruin a session TESTING = true and a non-empty FORCE_DOMAIN both look like a normal run from the outside. Check them before every participant.
            Then run the file, type the participant number and the session number at the prompts, and hand over the mouse.
            Running it

            What the participant sees·In order, per task

            1. 01Anchor question. Their budget, or the lowest wage they would accept. Typed, then ENTER.
            2. 02Attribute importance ratings. A sliding scale per attribute. For jobs, also how likely they would be to apply in each industry.
            3. 03Eye tracker calibration. Five points, follow the dot.
            4. 04Instructions, then comprehension checks. They cannot start until they answer correctly. This is deliberate.
            5. 05A practice episode. Saved and flagged as practice, so it is analysable or excludable later.
            6. 06The trials. Each one starts with a click on a centre crosshair, which is both a ready signal and a known gaze starting point.
            7. 07Drift checks. Every few trials, automatically.
            8. 08A summary screen. Then the next task in the session starts on its own.
            If the same domain runs twice in one sitting, steps 01 and 02 are skipped the second time. That is a cache, not a bug.
            Running it

            Your job during the session·Mostly not intervening

            • Sit where you can see the screen but they cannot see you. Being watched changes how long people look at things, which is our dependent measure.
            • Answer procedural questions, not strategic ones. "Which button rejects it?" is fine. "Should I bid high here?" gets "whatever feels right to you."
            • Watch the console, not the participant. Calibration warnings and drift-check failures print there.
            • Let timeouts happen. A timed-out trial is recorded as a timeout and handled in analysis. Prompting them corrupts it.
            Do step in when The screen is frozen or white, MATLAB has thrown an error, the participant says they feel unwell, or the tracker has clearly lost them for several trials in a row.
            A session that stutters, confuses someone, or strands them on a mis-click costs data, not just goodwill.

              After

              What gets written·Four files per run

              FileHolds
              <run>.matEverything. Anchor, attribute ratings, the stimulus window, regions of interest, the event log keyed to screen flip times.
              <run>.csvOne row per trial. This is what the analysis scripts read.
              <run>_gaze.matRaw gaze samples for the run.
              sub-XXXXX_manifest.matOne per participant. Every run they have done, with its status.

              Every file name carries the participant, session, task, domain, and timestamp, so nothing can collide and nothing needs renaming:

              sub-00012_ses-01_task-auction_dom-jobs_20260915_143205.csv
              Before they leave: open the session folder and confirm the run's files are there and are not zero bytes. The manifest should say complete, not started.
              After

              When it crashes·It will, eventually

              1. 01Do not panic in front of the participant. "Give me one second, the program has hiccuped" is enough.
              2. 02The crash is already saved. The error, the session, and the run all land in the Crashes folder automatically, and the manifest is marked crashed.
              3. 03Answer the prompt. If another task was scheduled, MATLAB asks whether to continue to it. Usually yes.
              4. 04If the screen is stuck, press Ctrl+C, then type sca and clear PsychImaging. Both, always. Closing the window alone leaves state behind that breaks the next run.
              5. 05Write down what was on screen. The crash file records the error, not what the participant was doing when it happened.
              To resume a participant later, run the same participant ID again. The manifest picks up where they left off.
              After

              Leave these alone·The short list

              • Anything in +utils/. That is the shared layer both tasks depend on.
              • The trial counts and timeouts in +utils/config.m. Changing them mid-collection splits the dataset into two studies.
              • The stimulus files. Job stimulus arms are selected by name at runtime, never by copying or renaming a file, because a rename severs the link to its provenance record.
              • Anything under data/. Participant data never goes into git.
              If something needs changing Say so rather than editing it. There is a second copy of this experiment on the lab share, and a change made in only one of them is worse than the problem it fixed.
              The switches at the top of run_battery.m are the only things you are expected to touch.
              After

              Practising and checking·Two commands

              Run both of these before your first real participant. The demo shows you every screen in a few minutes, and the analysis proves the data came out the way we think it did.

              %% MATLAB -- a short pass through every element
%% writes to Data_demo/, never to the real data folder
demo_battery
              # Terminal -- descriptive plots from that demo
Rscript analysis/R/run_all.R --data experiment/Data_demo

# or, with no MATLAB at all, on simulated data
Rscript analysis/R/run_all.R --simulate
              The analysis prints a data-integrity report before it plots anything: how many runs, how many trials, and which design cells are empty.
